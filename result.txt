Question: what deep nonlinear classifiers do ?

According to the provided context, deep nonlinear classifiers can fit their data very well (Source 4, page 1). This capability often leads network designers to include stochastic regularizers like adding noise to hidden layers or applying dropout to prevent overfitting (Source 4, page 1). The activation function choice is a necessary architecture decision for neural networks to ensure they function as deep nonlinear classifiers rather than deep linear classifiers (Source 4, page 1). Additionally, with proper regularization, deep nonlinear classifiers can behave like ensembles of networks (pseudoensembles), which can lead to marked accuracy increases (Source 4, page 1).

Question: what is Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)?

Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) is an Integral Probability Metric (IPM) defined as follows [Source 3]:

When the function class F_D is the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) H_k, i.e., F_D := {f ∈ H_k : ||f||_{H_k} ≤ 1}, the resulting IPM_D coincides with the maximum mean discrepancy:

MMD(p,π) = sup || E_{X∼p} f(X) - E_{X∼π} f(X) ||
          ||f||_{H_k} ≤ 1

MMD is used in GANs as a loss function [Source 3]. It is considered a weaker metric than 1-Wasserstein distance, and while MMD-based GANs still suffer from mode collapse issues, empirical results suggest they require smaller discriminative networks and enable faster training than Wasserstein-GANs [Source 3].

For theoretical guarantees, MMD requires certain kernel assumptions. Specifically, Assumption K2 (which states there exists a constant K_0 such that sup |k(x,x)| ≤ K_0) is used to derive error bounds for MMD(P_n, π) [Source 2]. Proposition 3.2 provides a high-probability non-asymptotic error bound for MMD, showing that for every τ > 0, with probability at least 1-2e^{-τ}:

MMD(P_n,π) ≤ 2√(2K_0/n) + 3√(K_0τ/n) [Source 1]

Many commonly used kernel functions satisfy the necessary assumptions for MMD, including the Gaussian kernel and inverse multiquadric (IMQ) kernel [Source 2].
